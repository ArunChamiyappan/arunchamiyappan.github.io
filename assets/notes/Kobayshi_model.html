Kobayashi Phase Field Model

The Kobayashi model (1993) is a classic phase-field model used to simulate dendritic crystal growth. It couples a phase-field equation for the order parameter \(p\) (where \(p=1\) is solid and \(p=0\) is liquid) with a heat diffusion equation for the temperature \(T\). The model incorporates anisotropy in the interfacial energy to produce realistic dendritic structures.

Phase Field Evolution Equation

The time evolution of the phase field variable \(p\) is governed by the following equation, which includes anisotropic gradient energy terms:

\[ \tau \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left( \epsilon^2 \nabla p \right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( \epsilon \frac{\partial \epsilon}{\partial \theta} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} \right) - \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \epsilon \frac{\partial \epsilon}{\partial \theta} \frac{\partial p}{\partial y} \right) + p(1-p)(p - \frac{1}{2} + m(T)) \]

where:

  • \(\tau\) is a relaxation time related to the interface mobility.
  • \(\epsilon(\theta)\) is the anisotropic gradient energy coefficient.
  • \(m(T)\) represents the thermodynamic driving force, which depends on the local undercooling.

Anisotropy

The anisotropy is introduced through the coefficient \(\epsilon\), which depends on the orientation angle \(\theta\) of the interface normal vector. The angle is given by \(\theta = \arctan(\partial_y p / \partial_x p)\).

The anisotropy function is typically defined as:

\[ \epsilon(\theta) = \bar{\epsilon} \left( 1 + \delta \cos(j(\theta - \theta_0)) \right) \]

where:

  • \(\bar{\epsilon}\) is the mean value of the gradient energy coefficient.
  • \(\delta\) is the strength of the anisotropy.
  • \(j\) is the mode number (e.g., \(j=4\) for four-fold symmetry).
  • \(\theta_0\) is the initial orientation angle.

Temperature Evolution Equation

The evolution of the dimensionless temperature \(T\) is described by the heat diffusion equation with a source term due to the latent heat of solidification:

\[ \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla^2 T + K \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} \]

where \(K\) is the dimensionless latent heat parameter.

Driving Force

The term \(m(T)\) couples the phase field to the temperature field and is given by:

\[ m(T) = \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \arctan(\gamma(T_{eq} - T)) \]

where \(\alpha\) and \(\gamma\) are positive constants, and \(T_{eq}\) is the equilibrium melting temperature (typically scaled to 1).
